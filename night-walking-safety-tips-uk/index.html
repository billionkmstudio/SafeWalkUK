Night walking safety tips for the UK

Most night walks end uneventfully, but a little preparation makes them easier and less stressful. These are general tips, not a guarantee, and nothing replaces your own judgement.

Before you set off

  • Plan your route. Compare options rather than defaulting to the shortest path. A slightly longer route along a lit, busy road is often preferable to a shortcut through a park or alley.
  • Check the time. Streets change character after pubs close and when shops shut. SafeWalk UK lets you compare the same route at different hours.
  • Charge your phone and keep it easy to reach.
  • Tell someone your route and expected arrival time. SafeWalk UK's Live Companion can share your location and ETA with a friend via a private link while you walk.

On the way

  • Stay on well-lit streets with other people or open businesses when you can.
  • Keep one ear free if you wear headphones, or keep the volume low.
  • Be aware of your surroundings and walk with purpose. Avoid looking at your phone for long periods.
  • Know where safe spaces are along the way, such as open shops, petrol stations, pharmacies and pubs.
  • Canal towpaths, alleys and car parks are often unlit and isolated at night. Consider alternatives.

If you feel unsafe

  • Trust your instincts. Cross the road, change direction, or head into an open shop or pub.
  • Many venues take part in the Ask for Angela scheme. You can ask staff discreetly for help if you feel unsafe.
  • In an emergency, call 999. If you cannot speak, you can call 999 from a mobile and press 55 when prompted, which tells the operator it is a genuine emergency. For non-emergencies, call 101.
Important: SafeWalk UK gives an experimental risk indicator from public data. A higher score does not guarantee safety. If you are in immediate danger, call 999.
